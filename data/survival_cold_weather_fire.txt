During a winter backpacking trip in wet alpine terrain, a hiker needed to start a fire before nightfall.
The environment involved sub-zero wind chill, damp wood, limited fuel, and reduced dexterity from cold hands.
The problem was inability to sustain ignition with standard tinder.
The solution was to build a raised dry platform, shave inner bark and resin-rich twigs, and shield the flame with a wind barrier.
The mechanism was isolating combustion materials from ground moisture and preserving initial heat concentration.
The result was reliable ignition in under ten minutes; however, fuel consumption increased because more prep material was used.
